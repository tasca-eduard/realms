# Realms: what they share, what each brings

A comparison of the two built realms, The Moonlit Keep (`castle`) and Whisperwood (`forest`), made on
2026-10-01 to see whether the game is on the right track before later realms are planned. The numbers
come from the code (both maps built with the game's own code and measured), the tests and BOARD.md's
measured runs, and 28 screenshots. The work that came out of it is in BOARD.md. Realm 3, the Sunken
Reef (`aqua`), was measured against both the same way on 2026-10-02, after its content round (its section
below; the counts from its map built with the game's code, 9 screenshots).

## The aim: about 40% common, 60% unique

Asked for on 2026-10-01: each realm about **40% common**, so the player knows where to go and what to
expect, and about **60% its own**: terrain, foes, mechanics and the rest.

Read that way, the common part is a realm's *grammar*: the shape of the quest (village, leader, the way,
the lever, the garrison, the tyrant), the village's services, the resting places and the rewards. The
unique part is its *vocabulary*: the land, the foes, the realm's own rules, its look and its music.

How it is scored: each part of a realm counts 1 if a player who finished the realm before would call it
the same thing (a recolour counts as the same), ½ if it has the same role in a clearly new form, 0 if
it is new. An estimate: read the shares as ±10.

Other measures of the same thing:
- 36 of realm 2's 54 placed foes are realm-1 types (67%); 46 stand on realm-1 bodies (85%). Only the
  spitter and the Warden run new AI.
- The ground-type mix overlaps 57%; realm 2 has no ground type of its own. 15 of its 29 regions have a
  realm-1 counterpart.
- In screenshots about 45% of the picture looks familiar; the colour of the light does most of the
  separating (realm 1's shots sit at hue 209-253°, realm 2's at 150-168°).
- Realm 2 plays 8 of realm 1's 9 music tracks and adds none; 69 of its 73 sound effects are realm 1's.
- The user's feedback on realm 2 never said it was too similar: every complaint asked for realm 1's
  quality (real zones, a purpose everywhere, nothing man-made in the wild). The land is where that work
  went, and it is the most original part. Foes and music got no such pass.

How it works today:
- Foes take as many blows with the sword the knight brings as realm 1's took with a new one (`foeHp`).
- Every hit costs one heart, in every realm, from every source.
- Each tyrant is tuned by hand, so a player-like bot wins in about a minute at the expected sword.
- Each realm's chests, quests and trial pay for what it sells, with a modest surplus.

So realm 2 is harder **in kind**, not in numbers: snares, lobbed seeds, hidden traps, timed thorns and a
tyrant that keeps away, while groups, elites and effect-causing foes went down. Upgrades mattered less:
they cut realm 1's work by 29% but realm 2's by 3% (the level-5 temper changed no placed foe's blows), so
the tempers now add +25% a level, as sharpening does.

Realm 3 is harder **in kind** again, and in amount: about a third more opening blows than realm 1 on arrival
(278 against 206, of them 56 for its two mini-bosses), its threats mostly up close (ranged foes 13%, from
36-41%), and air, floating and currents to fight in. Upgrades matter less than ever: +25% of the base is
+11% on what a level-5 sword does, and the two coral edges cut its work by 6%.

In formulas (`src/game/player.ts`, `enemies.ts`, `game.ts`):
```
sword damage   D(L) = 1 + 0.25·L   (the tempers past level 3 were +0.15 until 2026-10-01)
foe health     base · foeHp (x3 elite, x1.5 golden); tyrants not scaled
the realm rule ceil(base·foeHp / D(L on arrival)) = ceil(base / D(0)), so foeHp ≈ D(L on arrival)
hearts         5 + one per set of 3 shards + relics; flasks at most 6, 2 hearts each (3 with glowing bait)
prices         sword [80, 150, 240, 400, 560, 640, 720] by level; flask 60 + 40·(max - 3)
```

Where today's rules run out:
1. Hearts rise every realm while every hit still costs one heart: 6, 8 and 9 by the ends of realms 1 to 3,
   and realm 3's glowing bait makes each flask heal 3. Later realms get easier unless something else rises.
2. The sword's price list stops at level 7 (realm 3's coral-smith added 640 and 720); realm 4 needs more
   levels, or another kind of upgrade.
3. A level adds +25% of the base (the tempers were +15%, which rarely changed a blow count), so less and
   less of what the sword already does: +11% at level 6, +10% at level 7.
4. Flasks reach their cap of 6 in realm 1 (realm 3 makes each heal more instead).
5. Coins: each village now sells wares of its own (src/game/wares.ts: barding in Keepsfoot, boots and a
   tonic in Hollowbough, an air bladder and a lodestone, the prototype's coin magnet, on the reef); the
   combo keeper is left. Realm 3's content round paid about twice what its village sells (group 36).
6. Tyrants and mini-bosses are set by hand, and the code names each one.
7. The realm order isn't enforced (menu travel), so the sword a realm assumes isn't guaranteed: Whisperwood
   at level 0 is 46% more work, the Sunken Reef at level 3 37% (380 opening blows against 278).

## For later realms (notes, not a plan)

Written after realm 2, and what realm 3 did with each:
- The common part was "whatever realm 1 had", copied whole: the same map size and direction, the same
  counts of moonfires, shards, cages and waves, even the same lines. Decide the common 40% on purpose
  (likely the quest's shape, the village's services, rest and rewards) and let the rest change. *Realm 3's
  plan named it, and its frame (size, direction, counts) is its own.*
- A realm's foes should be mostly its own: most of what the player fights, not one creature in a crowd of
  recoloured goblins. *Under the sea they are; a third of its placed foes are still realm 1's goblins, recoloured.*
- Each realm should sound like itself. The prototype had a track and an ambience per realm (`o40.js`
  TRACKS, `r22.js` THEMES_AMB). *Realm 3 had its own music and ambience from its first group.*
- Each border opens with the beast freed in the realm before; give that beast something to open. *The
  Thornstag's second leap clears the Sea Stair's rockfall; the serpent will swim the Dune Strait.*
- Difficulty: revisit hit damage, the sword list, flasks and what coins buy before realm 3. *The sword
  list and the wares grew and flasks heal more; hit damage is unchanged.*

What worked in realm 3 (about 48% common on its first build; realm 2 was 67%, 58% after a follow-up):
- Planning the 40/60 from the start, and building the realm's own ground, props, foes and music in its
  first groups instead of fixing them afterwards.
- A frame of its own: another size, the journey running the other way and downward, other counts.
- One rule at the realm's heart (water: the suit, air, floating, currents, bubble columns) that changes how
  the shared moveset plays without changing the moveset.
- The realm's tool won from a mini-boss (Brassbelly's suit): a step of the quest that is the realm's own.
- Its fights built to the fair-fight rules from the start: lines and rings that fill before the blow, one
  attack at a time, air a nuisance and never a killer.

What to watch:
- **Recoloured foes.** The crew on the strand, the isles and before the palace are realm 1's goblins,
  shield goblins, archers and a brute in teal (21 of 62 placed foes), as are all of the trial's 11 and
  the Tidelord's calls. Dressing them as the realm's own (as group 24 dressed the Old Wood's) would take
  foes from about 54% to about 37%, and the realm to about 46%.
- **The light.** Realm 3's night is the Keep's blue (screens at hue 199-206°, realm 1's at 205-253°): its
  sea and sand set it apart, its light doesn't, and its first screens (down the Sea Stair to its foot:
  grass terraces under a blue moon, the sea out of view) read as the Keep's. A realm's night should differ
  in hue, and its first screen should say which realm it is.
- **The shared grammar thinned where it should hold.** The main quest no longer sends the knight to the
  village or its leader (its first step points straight at the lighthouse isle), and the crew before the
  palace open nothing. Decide whether "village, leader, garrison" is part of the common 40%.
- **Volume grows with each realm**: chests 11, 23, 33; people 7, 17, 34; quests 7, 5, 11. Realm 3's
  content round paid about twice what its village sells until group 36. Set the counts and the purse
  before a content round, and check them after it.
- **Upgrades fade.** Each sword level adds less of what the knight has (+10% at level 7) and the list ends
  at 7; hearts reach 9. Realm 4 needs another kind of upgrade, or hits that cost more.
- **Ranged foes nearly gone** (13%, from 36-41%): under the sea the threats come up close. Keep the mix
  varied: it's what made realm 2 harder in kind.
