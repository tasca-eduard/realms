# The Moonlit Keep (realm 1)

Realm 1 (`castle`): its places from the King's Road to the keep, its people, how its paths lead, its light, water
and life, and its edges.

- **The King's Road**: where you arrive, with the warhorse. The Old Warden's homestead
  stands in the meadow by the road (he has a job for you: raiders on the southern fields).
  Light the moonfire at the wayshrine; if you fall, you rise at the last moonfire you lit.
- **Keepsfoot**: the village. The road from the bridge climbs into a short street past the
  smithy's open forge to the square: the tavern (the innkeeper sells Moon Flasks), the Elder's
  hall, the smith (he sharpens your sword, and sells barding: each piece one more hit for your
  mount), the market stall, and the chapel by the north road. Paper lanterns (warm, moon-blue,
  violet) hang on nine ropes over the square and the street.
- **Keepsfoot's people** (21, 18 of them at work or play): Old Hob of the Watch walks his round
  over the bridge with a lantern; Garrow hammers at his anvil, sparks flying, while Wat his boy
  works the bellows; Nell and Dickon chase round the well; Old Aldous and Old Mabel sit out on the
  bench; Jory the Lamplighter goes from lamp to lamp with his pole; Osric angles off the bridge;
  Edda's washing hangs on the stream bank; Rufus drinks outside the tavern door; Bess feeds the
  hens behind Pip's house; Agnes minds the stall; Godric carts sacks to the tavern; Cole of the
  Village Watch paces the foot of the north road; Cuthbert sweeps the chapel step; Alys carries
  water. Walk past someone and they turn and say a word (one at a time); stand still and nobody
  does. The village follows the story: once Tam is home a feast table stands before the tavern,
  Tam at its head; once the drawbridge is down, ten lantern posts light the north road toward
  Blackpine; at dawn everyone is out in the square. Their talk takes in each piece of news.
- **Blackpine Wood and Gnasher's Camp**: someone is locked in a cage there. The camp is at its
  business until it sees you: a boar on the spit, two goblins dicing on a crate, one at the war
  drum.
- **The Outer Bailey**: the winch that lowers the drawbridge (arrow slits watch it).
- **The Moonlit Keep**: clear the courtyard garrison to open the great hall and the
  Goblin King. His crashes can bring the chandeliers down, on you or on him. The keep flies
  moon-blue banners and pennants with the gold crescent, and the beacon on the gatehouse burns
  cold moon-blue while the King holds it, gold at dawn.
- **Off the road**: the Seven Stones (a three-wave trial for the Knight's Crest relic and 90
  coins; its foes drop nothing, and falling resets it; the last wave is a brute, a shaman, a
  goblin and an elite boar; the stones' runes wake one after another round the ring, and all burn
  once the trial is won), the eighth stone, never raised, half sunk in the grass north-east of the
  ring with a chest under it, the
  Old Lodge (an elite beast), the Barrow Fields and its graveyard, the Overlook (a ruined
  watch post up the Pilgrims' Stair), the Hollow (a cave in the cliff below it),
  Mirrormere with its pier, the Sallow Marsh, the raided farm, a goblin camp on the
  river bank, the gorge lookout, and the Kings' Orchard gone wild behind the keep's west wall,
  in blossom (eleven trees, petals drifting, windfalls, old bee skeps).
- **The Old Mill**, on the stream above the ford: its wheel turns in a stone-lined race and
  throws foam, its window is lit, and a lane leads up from the ford to Hobb the Miller.
- **The First Knights' Isle**, out in Mirrormere where the first knights swore to keep the road: a
  moonlit stone knight among broken arches, a lore stone and a chest, reached by stepping stones
  hidden in the shallows. A night fisher's lantern boat drifts round it.
- **Pilgrims' Fall**: a spring pours off the heights at the Overlook's far (west) edge into a
  plunge pool; there is a ledge behind the falling water, and a chest on it.
- **The raided farm** smoulders while its raiders hold it. Beat them and go on your way, and
  when you come back it is being mended: a new frame and scaffolding on the barn, lanterns, and
  Wat and Edda Harrow back at work.
- **Chests and lore**: 14 chests and 6 lore stones in all.
- **Moon Shards**: three are hidden: by still water, behind old stone, above a long
  drop. All three give an extra heart. A cracked wall breaks to a heavy blow (finisher,
  full spin, dash strike or plunge).
- **Power-ups** (20 seconds) come from chests, elites and the odd pot: Fire Blade,
  Wind Boots (double jump), Magnet, Bubble (blocks two hits), Giant Slash. Golden foes
  are rare and drop ten times the coins.

**Finding your way.** Roads and trodden footpaths link every place with a purpose: the
village, the camps, the keep, the homestead, the stones, the farms, the ford, the mill, the
pier and the Overlook stair. Places that are there to be found (the Sallow Marsh, the First
Knights' Isle, the Hollow, Pilgrims' Fall) have no path: leave the track to find them.

**Light.** Each place has its own light, blended over a few metres where places meet, so you
see the next place's colour before you walk into it; lamps, fires and glowing things keep their
own. Keepsfoot and its tavern are warm amber (the homestead and the mill a little of that
warmth); Blackpine, the Old Lodge, Gnasher's Camp, the gorge lookout and the thorn road
green-black; the Barrow Fields a cold violet, their ghost-cyan lights bright against it; the
Seven Stones and the Knights' Isle silver with a cyan edge; the Hollow a cold blue-cyan; the Sallow Marsh sickly
yellow-green under thick mist; the fields, the King's Road, the raided farm and the Overlook
silver-blue; Mirrormere, the river and Pilgrims' Fall silver; the keep and its bailey indigo
against the torches' orange; the hall of the Moon Throne ember-red; the Kings' Orchard
moon-silver. Moonpetals, the realm's own flower, glow moon-blue in drifts inside the Seven
Stones, among the barrows and by the dolmen, and under the orchard's trees. At dawn the stone
turns rose-gold, the meadows green and the water silver.

**Water.** The stream runs (past the village, under the bridge and through the ford), as do the
Mirrow, the mill race and the spring above Pilgrims' Fall: ripples and foam drift downstream,
and there is white water at the ford, under the mill wheel and round bridges over running
water. Mirrormere, the moat and the marsh pools lie still. Low banks of earth, grass and sand
slope down into the water with a wandering waterline; stone walls, paving and wood stand sheer.
Shallows are clear over stones, silt and weed; deep water is dark and mirrors the sky. The
moon's path lies on the water toward you, and lamps and torches lie on it in wavering streaks.
Reeds grow in clumps in the still shallows and low stones stand in the running ones, never where
a path, a jetty or a bridge meets the water.

**Life.** None of it fights. Crows wheel round the donjon, the towers and the gatehouse, half of
them sitting on the battlements, and go up together when you come below; bats flit over the
barrows and out of the Hollow; mute swans on Mirrormere run along the water and take off when
you come, and mallards dabble there, on the moat and on the marsh pools; a grey heron hunts the
ford and the stream; frogs sit on the lily pads of the marsh and Mirrormere and leap off; roach
rise in rings. Flocks of black-faced sheep graze the meadows round the Seven Stones and south of
the barrows and make off together, two cows stand by the homestead, and white geese graze the
stream's banks. Now and then the white hart, its antlers glowing moon-blue, stands in a glade of
Blackpine, among the barrows or by the Seven Stones, and bolts. In the air: moths at every warm
lamp and over the grass, thistledown over the fields, midges over the water, glow-worms on
Blackpine's floor, sallow lights over the marsh, warm motes at the tavern, and pale smoke from
the chimneys.

There are no invisible walls. Past the old map edge the land goes on until something
real stops you: mountain cliffs to the north and west, a gorge east of Blackpine (fall in
and you lose a heart), the deep Mirrow river along the east and south (the King's Road
bridge is broken), and Mirrormere to the west. Clear shallow water can be waded (slowly);
dark deep water can't be entered or jumped across. The stream has a ford in the fields.
